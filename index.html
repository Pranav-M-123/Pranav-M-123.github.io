01 / THE PERSON BEHIND THE PROJECTS

Curiosity is my starting point.

I am a Computer Engineering student at the Georgia Institute of Technology, where I am exploring the space between the physical hardware that makes computing possible and the software that gives it purpose.

What excites me most about engineering is the ability to connect ideas across disciplines. A processor isn't just an arrangement of logic gates; it is a foundation for the applications, devices, and experiences people depend on. A web application isn't just a polished interface; behind it are data models, decisions about performance, and countless opportunities to make a system more reliable and useful.

My work reflects that curiosity. Through SiliconJackets, I have explored processor architecture and digital design with SystemVerilog and engineering CAD tools. Through my software projects, I have worked with databases, asynchronous updates, authentication, and user interfaces. As part of Georgia Tech's EcoCAR VIP team, I am learning how connected and automated vehicle technologies bring software, sensing, simulation, and safety considerations together.

Engineering also means understanding people. My experience teaching mathematics, tutoring students, and helping lead an adaptive dance organization has shaped the way I approach difficult problems. I have learned to communicate clearly, adapt to different needs, and build with the end user in mind. Whether I am explaining a concept or debugging a design, I try to combine technical rigor with patience and empathy.

I'm still early in my engineering journey, and that is part of what makes it exciting. I want to keep building, keep asking better questions, and contribute to teams working on meaningful systems.

01 / HARDWARE · SYSTEMVERILOG

32-bit RISC-V Processor

Understanding a computer by building its logic.

In SiliconJackets' digital design onboarding, I worked on a single-cycle RV32I processor in SystemVerilog. My work involved fundamental modules such as the controller, register file, and immediate generator, as well as understanding the flow of instructions and data through a processor. I used remote engineering infrastructure and Cadence SimVision to inspect digital behavior and support debugging.

The most valuable lesson was learning to reason about the relationship between an architecture diagram and an actual hardware implementation. Decisions about control signals, immediate formats, and data movement become concrete when the design must behave correctly under simulation.

02 / FULL-STACK · PYTHON

Smart Microblog

A social feed with sentiment-aware interaction.

I built a full-stack microblogging application using Flask, SQLAlchemy, TextBlob, JavaScript, and CSS. The application analyzes the sentiment of user posts and uses that result to change how content is presented visually. AJAX polling supports updates without requiring users to refresh the entire page, while Werkzeug password hashing supports safer account handling.

This project taught me to think about the entire experience, not only the backend. Database design, asynchronous communication, authentication, and visual feedback all have to work together. I also explored scroll-reveal animation and glassmorphism-inspired UI design to make the interface feel more responsive and intentional.

03 / WEB APPLICATION · DATA MODELING

Community Appointment Scheduler

Making peer-to-peer tutoring easier to coordinate.

I designed a web application to centralize tutoring appointments and educational resources. Built with Python, Flask, SQLite, and Bootstrap, the project uses a relational database to represent scheduling relationships and SQL JOIN queries to produce different dashboard views for students and instructors.

One design challenge was supporting users who could participate in more than one role. That required thinking carefully about data relationships and interface expectations rather than treating every account as a single fixed user type. The project strengthened my understanding of how good data modeling makes an application more flexible as its requirements evolve.
